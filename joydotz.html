Overview

I rebuilt the Joydotz brand and Shopify site as the only designer on the project, turning a default store into a soft, collectible identity made for Gen Z.

Joydotz makes hydrocolloid spot patches in flower, cloud and butterfly shapes. Through Swedish Falls LLC, it was repositioning for the US market, where its old site still read as a generic acne treatment.

I owned the color system, product and site visuals, interaction design and social content, working alongside the company's engineer, who handled the heavier coding.

The situation

Joydotz began as a spot-patch brand in China. By the time it decided to go overseas, its new packaging was already produced and shipped to the US, and only then did the team bring us in to rebuild the website. The original brief was to build the site in a soft nude, a tone close to the color of the patch itself.

The problem

I didn't think that palette could reach a Gen Z audience. Nude, skin-toned palettes read as luxury beauty, the visual language of foundation brands and sites like Acne Studios or Rhode, not a playful patch you want to collect.

The call

We confirmed with the factory that the packaging could not be changed or recalled, so I designed from it instead of against it: the site takes the pink and blue already on the boxes and turns them into a soft, dreamy world for the whole brand.

Homepage Hero Film

I wrote the outline for this film, with a colleague contributing ideas for the character designs. After studying how similar brands such as Flower Knows, Rhode and Dior stage their homepages, we wanted a more alive hero than a still banner, so I wrote the creative guideline and storyboard and we briefed a production team to make the film.

Concept

"A Little Joy Attack": friends sneak a Joydotz patch onto each other's faces. The product shows up in an action between people, never as a static ad.

Scenes × Shapes

Picnic for the flower, bracelet making and a grass-field run for the butterfly, baking and a rainy window for the cloud. The guideline asks for a patch on screen within the first five seconds.

Built for Silent Autoplay

Every beat reads without sound: someone notices a friend, a patch appears, it lands, they laugh. The logo ending loops straight back to the first scene.

Blind Box Characters

Unbuilt Concept

Developed with the Joydotz team: sell the patches as blind boxes, giving each of the three patch shapes (flower, cloud, butterfly) an original character in the style of Sailor Moon or My Little Pony.

Future product drops would become "skins" of those three starter characters rather than entirely new SKUs, modeled on Pop Mart's approach, to deepen brand storytelling for the Gen Z audience beyond "just a daily patch."

What's Next: Version 3

94%of visitors opened the site on a phone

Everything on this page is version 2, which we designed laptop first, with many hover effects. After launch, 94% of visitors arrived on mobile, most likely because our traffic came from influencer posts, Reels and TikTok. We're now building version 3 on top of version 2, designed mobile first so the moments that relied on hover still work on a phone.

Reflection

Impact

Took Joydotz from a default Shopify skin into a cohesive, interactive brand system built for its US Gen Z audience: a packaging-derived color palette, a shape-based product language, and a mascot moment that carries the identity to the footer.

Reflection

Working from an established brand rather than a blank page meant finding the fresh angle already hiding in the packaging, the color gradient, the shape language, instead of inventing one from scratch, then carrying it consistently across every touchpoint.
