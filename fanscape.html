04 — Fanscape

FAN★SCAPE

A map-based app that helps fans find, pin and share real-world fandom places.

UX / UI Design · Figma Academic Project · Dec 2025 Mobile App · Interactive Prototype

Overview

One map for every fandom place: concert sites, pop-ups, murals, idol cafés and photo spots.

Problem

Fandom places are scattered everywhere.

Problem

Fandom experiences are split across many platforms. Fans can't easily find real-world fandom places or see where their community has been.

Pain point

"I know this place exists, but I can't find it." Locations are buried in TikTok, Instagram, Reddit and Google Maps, often inaccurate, and gone fast.

How might we

give fans a centralized, trustworthy, map-based platform to explore fandom places, verified by fans, filtered by lenses and connected to their community?

Design Goal

Pin meaningful places through lenses.

Build a map-based experience that helps fans pin meaningful fandom locations through lenses.

Success looks like
  • Find a fandom spot fast, in any city
  • Lenses filter location types clearly
  • See friends' pins on map and profile
  • Fan-made guidebooks
Target users
  • Fans who want to remember places that matter to their fandom
  • International fans traveling to a new city
  • Fandom friend groups
Constraints
  • Locations rely on user content
  • Pins must scale without clutter
  • Privacy for friend visibility
  • Mobile-first, cross-cultural (US, Korea, China, Japan, Europe)

Research

Competitive analysis + 9 interviews.

01

Competitive analysis

Weverse · Weibo · Xiaohongshu · TikTok fan guides

  • Fandom communities are mostly virtual; no platform maps real-world fandom places
  • Platforms amplify emotion but lose the link to real places
02

Semi-structured interviews

6 fans · K-pop, C-pop, Western fandoms

  • Fandom journeys are spatial but poorly documented
  • Fans only care about their own fandom
  • Fans want to see where others pinned

→ Pins, "Select your fandom", Guidebooks

03

Follow-up interviews

3 fans · K-pop, Marvel, Broadway

  • Fans need a reliable way to verify real spots
  • Overloaded maps overwhelm; users need filtering

→ Five Lenses, Friends tab

Testing

4 rounds with fans, classmates, ITP and Design Lab.

Worked
  • Warm tone and mascot: "it's cute… the maps look really lovely"
  • Map as home, features as overlays
  • Lens filtering felt intuitive
  • "Welcome!" and "Sent!" felt rewarding
Didn't
  • Why are other fandoms' pins showing?
  • Friend page showed too much personal info
  • Likes and sharing were unclear
  • Full-page navigation lost context

Iterations

01Fandom Choice selector

Hides irrelevant fandom pins.

02Likes and share tab

Clearer icons, animated with variables.

03Friend page redesign

Only pins, fandom badges and a signature.

04Overlays, not pages

Users stay anchored to the map.

Reflection

Fun and function, together.

Learned

Users want emotion and function at once; the interface has to feel fun while solving a real problem.

Learned

Clear map logic (fandom choice, lenses) is essential; users expect instant feedback.

Learned

Fans read UI symbolically: icon shape, color and microcopy shape trust.
